# Ship 30 for 30 — Guide Notes

> Extracted from the official Ship 30 for 30 methodology (ship30for30.com) and supporting community resources (typeshare.co, medium.com guides).

## What Is an Atomic Essay?

An **Atomic Essay** is a concise, single-idea piece of writing designed to be published online. Popularized by the Ship 30 for 30 community, it sits between a social media post and a long-form blog article. (Source: ship30for30.com)

## Core Principles

1. **Single-Idea Focus**: Each essay explores only one idea. This keeps the writing manageable and ensures the message remains clear and punchy. (Source: ship30for30.com)

2. **Concise Length**: The standard atomic essay length is approximately **250 words**. For longer-form adaptations (as required by this brief), the same discipline applies — eliminate fluff, focus on essential points. (Source: ship30for30.com, typeshare.co)

3. **Data-Driven Iteration**: Atomic essays are tools for testing ideas. By publishing regularly, you gather feedback to see which topics resonate before investing in longer content like books or courses. (Source: ship30for30.com)

4. **Strong Hook**: Your opening line (the "lead-in") acts as a hook to draw readers into the essay. It must be attention-grabbing and promise a specific, useful insight. (Source: ship30for30.com)

5. **Narrative Progression**: The essay should follow a clear narrative arc — from hook to context to insights to takeaway — rather than being a random list of thoughts. (Source: typeshare.co writing guides)

6. **Structured Formatting**: Use headings, bullet points, and **selective bold** to make the essay skimmable. Short paragraphs and clear visual hierarchy improve engagement. (Source: ship30for30.com, typeshare.co)

7. **Specific Takeaway**: Every essay must end with one specific, useful, actionable takeaway the reader can apply immediately. Vague conclusions are unacceptable. (Source: ship30for30.com)

8. **Claims Grounded in Evidence**: All claims must be grounded in specific evidence (in our case, retrieved transcript chunks). No unsupported assertions. (Source: adapted from Ship 30 "data-driven" principle)

9. **Consistency over Perfection**: The goal is to build a writing habit. Ship the work rather than agonizing over perfection. Embrace "vomit drafts" and refine through iteration. (Source: ship30for30.com, stewartfenwick.com)

## The 6-Step Framework

1. **Define Your Topics**: Choose 3-5 broad topics (content buckets) you are interested in.
2. **Brain Dump Ideas**: Generate 10 ideas for each topic.
3. **Create a Schedule**: Organize ideas into a calendar.
4. **Use Templates**: Use templates like "3 Lessons Learned", "How to [Outcome]", or "The Problem with [Topic]" to structure arguments quickly.
5. **Write to the Length**: Keep drafts focused. Use simple language, short paragraphs, and clear formatting (bullet points, bold text).
6. **Hook and Publish**: Craft a compelling hook and publish. Gather engagement and data.

## Adaptation for Lenny Growth Assistant (~1,250 Words)

The assignment brief requires essays of approximately **1,250 words** (±10%, so 1,125–1,375 acceptable). This is longer than a standard 250-word atomic essay but the same principles apply at scale:

- **One core idea per essay**, explored in depth with multiple supporting angles from podcast transcripts
- **Strong hook** in the opening paragraph
- **Clear narrative progression** through sections with headings
- **Headings + bullets + selective bold** formatting throughout
- **Every claim grounded** in specific retrieved transcript chunks with citations
- **One specific useful takeaway** that the reader can act on immediately
- **Word count measured in code**, not estimated

## Key Difference from Standard Atomic Essays

The standard atomic essay is 250 words as a screenshot. Our adaptation maintains all Ship 30 principles but expands to ~1,250 words to allow:
- Deeper exploration of the single idea
- Multiple supporting examples from different podcast episodes
- Richer narrative structure with section headings
- More detailed grounding in transcript evidence
